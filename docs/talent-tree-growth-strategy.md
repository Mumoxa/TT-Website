# Talent Tree — website & growth strategy

## Positioning
**Talent Tree is a specialist recruitment partner for organisations making important, nuanced people decisions.** The brand should feel like a calm, commercially aware alternative to high-volume recruitment: research-led, candid and personal.

This strategy deliberately avoids unsupported claims, client names, placement metrics, awards and guarantees. Replace the marked evidence gaps with approved proof before publishing them as fact.

---

## 1. Ideal customer profile

### Primary buyer
**Hiring decision-maker at a South African or internationally connected business**—typically a founder, executive, HR/People leader, talent leader, or functional head—who owns the outcome of a specialist or leadership hire.

- **Organisation:** growth-stage company, established SME, professional-services firm, financial-services business, technology company, or a division within an enterprise.
- **Moment:** a critical role is vacant, growth has exposed a capability gap, a confidential replacement is needed, or the internal team lacks capacity / market reach.
- **Mindset:** values quality, speed with care, privacy, and a firm point of view more than the lowest apparent fee.
- **Decision criterion:** “Will this partner represent us credibly and give us a smaller set of candidates I can confidently meet?”

### Secondary audience
- Internal talent / HR teams who need an extension of capacity for a difficult brief.
- Leaders who need market information before approving a role or compensation range.
- Candidates assessing whether Talent Tree will handle their career confidentially and professionally.

### Key pain points
1. Great candidates are not applying to public advertisements.
2. Internal teams are overloaded and lack time for precise outreach.
3. Previous agencies supplied volume, but weak relevance and little context.
4. A bad hire is expensive, disruptive and visible internally.
5. The brief is not yet clear enough: scope, package and profile may not match the market.
6. Confidentiality and employer reputation are at risk during outreach.

### Buying triggers
- A senior / scarce-skills vacancy has been open too long.
- A business plan, transformation or product launch depends on one or more hires.
- A confidential resignation or replacement.
- Poor quality from an incumbent supplier.
- A newly approved headcount plan that requires market mapping.
- Leadership wants an independent view of candidate availability or salary reality.

### Likely objections and conversion answer
| Objection | Recommended answer |
| --- | --- |
| “We can advertise ourselves.” | Ads reach active job seekers; specialist search reaches the relevant passive market and brings qualification context. |
| “Agencies send too many unsuitable CVs.” | Promise process transparency and a concise, jointly-agreed shortlist standard—not a volume target. |
| “The fee feels high.” | Frame fee against leadership time, vacancy cost, missed opportunity and the cost of a poor hire. Show the work behind the outcome: discovery, mapping, outreach, assessment and stakeholder support. |
| “We need to move fast.” | Explain that early market intelligence prevents weeks of misdirected interviewing. Agree decision timelines and feedback cadence at kickoff. |
| “We must keep this private.” | Describe confidential-brief protocols, named points of contact and need-to-know candidate communication. |

### Desired outcome
A decision-maker wants to feel informed, represented and in control: a credible market view, a small shortlist of appropriate people, confidence in the decision and a process that protects their employer brand.

## 2. Recommended sitemap and user journey

### Core sitemap
1. **Home** — positioning, services overview, proof, process, enquiry.
2. **Services** — Specialist Search, Market Intelligence, Selective Team Builds; pricing approach and FAQs.
3. **Case Studies** — anonymised Challenge → Strategy → Execution → Results stories, subject to approval.
4. **About** — mission, values, leadership perspective and operating principles.
5. **Insights** — useful, non-generic hiring intelligence, salary/market guidance and leadership hiring content.
6. **Contact / Discuss a brief** — short qualification form and direct contact details.
7. **For Candidates** (recommended phase two) — candidate promise, current opportunities / registration, privacy expectations.
8. **Privacy policy / POPIA notice** — mandatory operational trust page.

### Navigation
Keep top navigation to: **Services · Approach · Case studies · About · Discuss a brief.** On mobile, use a clear menu drawer and retain the contact CTA. Avoid a large multi-level navigation; the buyer has a specific job to solve.

### Conversion journey
1. Visitor lands on a value proposition that distinguishes Talent Tree from CV-volume agencies.
2. They self-identify with a business-critical hiring challenge.
3. Services and process reduce uncertainty about what happens next.
4. Case-study structure proves strategic thinking without breaching client confidence.
5. FAQs address cost, confidentiality and fit.
6. The form asks only enough to start a relevant conversation.
7. Thank-you follow-up confirms response expectation and offers a valuable next step.

### Purpose of homepage sections
| Section | Conversion role |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | States who Talent Tree serves and what outcome it creates within seconds. The direct CTA reduces ambiguity. |
| “Judgement call” introduction | Names the buyer’s frustration with job-board / volume recruitment and introduces the strategic alternative. |
| Service list | Lets visitors quickly self-select their need without reading a long sales page. |
| Standard / point of view | Converts intangible quality into tangible behaviours: feedback, qualification and reputation protection. |
| Case cards | Establishes expertise through problem-solving structure while respecting confidential client work. |
| Process | Makes the premium service feel deliberate and dependable, rather than opaque. |
| About | Humanises the boutique model and explains why personal senior attention matters. |
| FAQ | Handles objections adjacent to the CTA, reducing form abandonment. |
| Contact form | Turns interest into a low-friction, relevant enquiry. |

## 3. Service page blueprint

### Page goal
Make a premium fee feel logical by showing the level of thinking, market work and reputational care required—not by competing on fee.

### Recommended order
1. **Hero:** “The hire is important. The search should be too.” Explain the target buyer and primary outcome.
2. **Problem:** Cost of a prolonged vacancy, poor signal and unclear market assumptions.
3. **Solutions:** Separate Specialist Search, Market Intelligence and Team Build offers.
4. **What is included:** Discovery workshop, role narrative, target-market mapping, tailored approach, qualification, shortlist context, interview / offer support and post-placement check-in. Only list activities Talent Tree actually performs.
5. **Process and client responsibilities:** timelines, feedback expectations, exclusivity / briefing requirements where applicable.
6. **Premium pricing strategy:** Avoid a public rate card for bespoke search. Use “engagement scoped to the complexity and importance of the brief,” then explain fee drivers: seniority, scarcity, geography, confidentiality and research depth. Add a “Request an engagement outline” CTA.
7. **Proof:** Approved testimonial(s), results or anonymised examples. Do not use invented performance figures.
8. **Risk reversal:** A clear written service standard, check-in cadence and transparent milestones. Only offer a replacement guarantee if the business can operationally honour it and legal terms are reviewed.
9. **FAQs:** expected timeline, candidate exclusivity, confidentiality, fees, candidate ownership and what a strong brief includes.
10. **CTA:** “Discuss a role” / “Ask for a market view.”

## 4. Case study structure

Use a consistent **Challenge → Strategy → Execution → Results** format. Keep identities anonymous until clients approve attribution.

### Case study template
- **Title:** Outcome-led, specific but confidential. Example: “A clearer path to a specialist commercial leader.”
- **Context:** sector, company stage and confidentiality status.
- **Challenge:** What made this difficult? Include the business consequence of delay.
- **Strategy:** How Talent Tree defined the ideal profile and search map.
- **Execution:** Research, outreach, calibration, stakeholder management and candidate experience work.
- **Results:** use approved measurable outcomes such as shortlist timing, offer acceptance, retention milestone or team capability impact. State the measurement period and source.
- **Client voice:** a signed, attributable quote where possible.
- **Credibility rail:** approved client logo, certification, industry association, media mention or award. Do not imply any that do not exist.
- **CTA:** “Facing a similar brief? Discuss it confidentially.”

### Visual direction
Use abstract market-map graphics, editorial typography, cropped detail imagery and simple data callouts rather than stock handshake photos. Where client images are used, secure rights and keep the visual system consistent.

## 5. About page blueprint

### Page angle
The page should make the customer’s future the main character: Talent Tree exists to help organisations make better people decisions. Biography supports credibility but does not lead.

### Content hierarchy
1. Mission: a concise statement about better, more human specialist hiring.
2. Customer transformation: from uncertainty and noise to clarity and a confident decision.
3. Values: judgement over volume; candour over convenience; relationships over transactions; discretion by default.
4. Unique approach: small senior team, deliberate research and honest market conversation—verify each point before publishing.
5. Expertise and achievements: years in business (existing site states 2013), actual sector expertise, approved certifications / memberships / media mentions.
6. Personality: concise founder / team narrative and a high-quality portrait only if it feels current and authentic.
7. CTA: “Bring us your difficult brief.”

## 6. Design system recommendations

### Design direction
**Editorial precision meets human intelligence.** The live website implements a premium dark-ink / paper palette, cyan technology signal and muted green growth cue. It should feel considered, grounded and modern—never corporate-generic or overly decorative.

### Tokens
- **Ink:** `#092539` — primary text / authority.
- **Deep ink:** `#0E3044` — dark surfaces.
- **Paper:** `#F4F1EA` — primary background.
- **Cream:** `#FBF9F4` — light contrast surface.
- **Signal blue:** `#019AD4` — primary actions and interactive highlights.
- **Growth mint:** `#B9D5CA` — supporting surface.
- **Gold:** `#E8C45A` — only for sparing accent / case distinction.

### Type
- Headlines: high-contrast editorial serif (currently Playfair Display) for confidence and emotional specificity.
- Body: legible humanist sans (currently DM Sans).
- Labels / metadata: restrained monospace (currently DM Mono).
- Maintain body text at 16px or larger and contrast at WCAG AA or stronger.

### Interaction
- Buttons: simple square-corner utility buttons; one bright primary action per view.
- Cards: restrained 1–3px lift and surface tint on hover; never rely on colour alone to communicate clickability.
- FAQ: animated expansion, plus / close indicator, `aria-expanded` state.
- Motion: 150–300ms ease transitions. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` in the next production pass.
- Images: favour real people, authentic working environments and abstract talent-map motifs. Do not use AI portraits as client/team imagery or generic office stock as proof.

## 7. SEO and technical recommendations

### Priority on-page SEO
- Create unique title tags and 150–160 character meta descriptions for every primary page.
- Suggested home title: **Specialist Recruitment & Talent Search | Talent Tree South Africa**
- Suggested home description: **Talent Tree helps ambitious organisations make high-stakes specialist hires with market insight, discretion and carefully qualified shortlists.**
- Use one descriptive H1 per page, semantic H2 structure and descriptive internal anchor text.
- Add location relevance naturally: Cape Town, Johannesburg, South Africa—only where Talent Tree genuinely serves.
- Use descriptive image alt text; decorative images should have empty alt text.
- Add Open Graph social cards and a favicon / web manifest.

### Schema markup
Validate using Google’s Rich Results Test after implementation:
- `Organization` / `ProfessionalService` with official name, logo, telephone, email, address and sameAs links.
- `WebSite` with SearchAction only when site search exists.
- `Service` for each genuine offer.
- `FAQPage` only for FAQs visibly shown on the page.
- `Article` / `BlogPosting` for insights.
- Never use `Review` or aggregate-rating schema without genuine reviews that meet Google policy.

### Technical quality
- Convert the logo to optimised SVG or transparent WebP after confirming brand approval; the current PNG is large.
- Self-host font files or use a compliant font delivery plan to reduce render blocking and privacy considerations.
- Serve responsive, compressed images in AVIF/WebP, explicit width / height and lazy loading below the fold.
- Target Core Web Vitals: LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms and CLS under 0.1 on mobile.
- Add a real form endpoint, server-side validation, spam protection and a consent notice before production.

## 8. Lead generation, analytics & content

### Lead magnets
1. **The specialist hiring brief scorecard** — a short downloadable checklist for role clarity, decision criteria and candidate narrative.
2. **South African specialist talent market snapshot** — quarterly, fact-checked observations on availability and hiring friction.
3. **Salary conversation guide** — only publish ranges sourced from Talent Tree’s real data and with scope / methodology.

Use an inline email capture in Insights and an exit-intent alternative only after sufficient traffic. Do not place disruptive pop-ups in the first visit journey.

### Email nurture
- **Immediately:** send requested resource and set expectations.
- **Day 3:** “Three signs the brief needs market calibration.”
- **Day 7:** anonymised case story.
- **Day 14:** invitation to a confidential 20-minute market conversation.
- Segment candidates separately from client leads and comply with POPIA consent and unsubscribe requirements.

### Analytics & conversion tracking
Implement cookie consent before non-essential tags. Then configure GA4 and/or a privacy-conscious analytics platform with:
- `generate_lead` — successful enquiry submission.
- `click_email` — mailto click.
- `cta_click` — CTA label and page context.
- `faq_open` — question text.
- `case_study_view` and `lead_magnet_download`.
- Funnel: landing → service view → CTA → form start → form complete.

Connect form leads to the CRM with source, landing page, campaign parameters and service interest. Test tracking in debug mode before launch.

### A/B testing ideas (one hypothesis at a time)
1. Hero CTA: “Discuss a brief” vs. “Get a market view.”
2. Hero value proposition: outcome-led vs. pain-led message.
3. Short form vs. form with role / budget / timeline qualification.
4. Case studies before vs. after the process section.
5. “No-obligation consultation” wording vs. a more specific “20-minute market conversation.”

Avoid testing until baseline conversion volume is sufficient; otherwise qualitative user interviews and session reviews are more informative.

### 6-month editorial plan
Publish one high-quality article per month, each connected to a service CTA:
- Writing a specialist hiring brief candidates respond to.
- When to map the market before opening a role.
- The hidden cost of a prolonged critical vacancy.
- How to protect candidate experience during confidential hiring.
- How leaders can assess commercial judgement in interview.
- Building a team without lowering the hiring bar.

Each article should link to the relevant service and one supporting insight. Update factual hiring-market pieces quarterly.

## 9. Conversion review: highest-impact next improvements

### Priority 1 — before production launch
1. **Connect the form to a secure CRM / email workflow.** The current website provides a frontend thank-you interaction only; it does not send the enquiry. Add server-side validation, spam prevention, delivery monitoring and a clear success / error state.
2. **Add real proof.** Secure 2–4 approved testimonials, anonymised but measurable case results, and verified client / association / media evidence. The current design intentionally does not fabricate endorsements.
3. **Optimise the logo asset.** Confirm transparent source artwork. The current supplied PNG is visually retained, but an oversized raster logo is not ideal for performance or crispness.
4. **Publish privacy / POPIA documentation.** Link it from the form and footer.
5. **Add metadata, social sharing data and schema.** Essential for discoverability and professional sharing.

### Priority 2 — conversion improvement
6. Add an explicit candidate path so job-seekers do not compete with client enquiries.
7. Create a dedicated Services page for paid-search / SEO landing intent.
8. Add calendar booking only if the team can reliably honour availability; otherwise preserve the personal enquiry model.
9. Test form length and CTA language after tracking is live.

### Priority 3 — scale
10. Build the Insights hub and one genuine lead magnet.
11. Add approved case-study detail pages.
12. Use customer interview recordings and lost-deal feedback to improve objection copy.

## 10. 90-day action plan

### Days 1–30: establish trust and measurement
- Confirm positioning, target sectors, real service scope and voice with leadership.
- Approve claims, testimonials, case-study permissions, legal copy and contact details.
- Connect form to CRM and email notification; test success, failure and spam paths.
- Implement consent, analytics events, Search Console and XML sitemap.
- Optimise logo, fonts, images and metadata; run mobile performance and accessibility checks.
- Conduct five short interviews with recent clients / prospects to validate buying language.

### Days 31–60: deepen proof and capture demand
- Publish a complete Services page and two approved case studies.
- Launch a brief scorecard lead magnet plus first nurture sequence.
- Create a candidate pathway and privacy / POPIA page.
- Implement Organization, Service and FAQ schema where eligible.
- Start an initial CTA / form test only after baseline tracking confirms correct firing.

### Days 61–90: build compounding visibility
- Publish two useful, evidence-backed insight pieces with internal links to services.
- Review funnel data, form drop-off and qualitative sales feedback.
- Refine copy where objections cluster; add only verified proof.
- Improve local / sector SEO pages only where Talent Tree can provide genuinely distinct content.
- Establish a quarterly process for refreshing case studies, insights, talent-market data and conversion hypotheses.

**Success measures:** qualified enquiries, enquiry-to-first-conversation rate, first-conversation-to-engagement rate, service-page conversion rate, organic non-brand impressions, Core Web Vitals and lead quality—not raw traffic alone.
